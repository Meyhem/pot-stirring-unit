# Stirrer – automatic pot stirrer (3D-printed)

DIY stirrer that stirs food in a pot on the stove. Nothing but the gearbox and
the stirring shaft sits above the food.

## The whole unit (top → bottom)
1. **Gearbox (top, above the pot)** – `angled_gearbox.scad`. Bevel gear pair in a
   split housing. Takes the tilted input rod and turns it into a vertical output shaft.
2. **Output shaft + paddle** – 6 mm stainless rod straight down into the pot, wooden
   paddle at the end (paddle not modelled yet).
3. **Input rod** – 6 mm steel rod running at 30° from vertical, from the motor up
   to the gearbox.
4. **Holding arm = spine** – wooden 20×40 mm arm standing on edge (40 mm in the shaft
   plane = the plane gravity bends it in), parallel to the input rod. Its near face is
   `arm_y0` = 31.5 mm from the rod axis (Ø55 drill chuck radius + 4 mm). A printed
   foot holds its bottom end on the board at 60°; the motor cradle and the gearbox
   both grip it with cheeks + wood screws → motor and gearbox aligned by construction.
   Not a printed part (plain wood), shown as a ghost in the assemblies.
5. **Motor mount (bottom, beside the pot)** – `motor_mount.scad`. Cradle hanging under
   the arm + 2 clamp caps for the TOPOWER 550-18V motor (RS-550, Ø38 mm body), plus
   the arm foot. A **drill chuck** (Ø55 at its thickest, length unmeasured – model
   assumes 90) on the motor shaft grips the 6 mm input rod directly (no coupler).

## Files
- `common.scad` – parameters shared by both models: `shaft_angle`, chuck, arm size +
  `arm_y0`, cheek thickness, wood screw pattern + `arm_screw_cut()`. Plain OpenSCAD.
  Change arm/chuck/angle ONLY here, then re-export both models.
- `angled_gearbox.scad` – parametric gearbox (current version: v4). Needs BOSL2 + common.scad.
- `motor_mount.scad` – motor cradle on the arm, clamps, arm foot. Plain OpenSCAD + common.scad.
- `stl/` – exported parts at default parameters: gearbox (housing_a/b, gear_in/out,
  spacer) and motor mount (motor_cradle, motor_clamp ×2 to print, arm_foot).
  OpenSCAD re-exports can reorder triangles without changing geometry – don't commit
  an STL whose sorted vertices are unchanged.
- `stirrer.3mf` – old slicer project (2026-11-20), predates the arm/motor redesign.
  Re-slice from `stl/` before printing.
- `README.md` – public overview with images (GitHub landing page).
- `img/` – README images. All except `gear_boss.png` (carried over from the chat)
  are regenerated by `./render_images.sh`.
- `prompt_regenerate_gearbox.md` – short spec (Slovak) to regenerate the gearbox from scratch.

## Gearbox (angled_gearbox.scad) – current design
- Straight bevel pair (BOSL2 `bevel_gear`, spiral=0), shaft angle 30° (input tilted
  30° from vertical), module 2, 15T pinion / 29T output gear (~1.93:1), backlash 0.2.
  Pitch dia 58 / 30 mm, pitch angles 19.87° / 10.13°.
- 6 mm steel shafts, each in two 626 bearings (6×19×6), 18 mm apart.
  Pockets Ø19.3 × 6.4 mm (`brg_clr`, `brg_hclr`) – 6.0 mm pockets were too tight in a real print.
  Pocket ledge touches only the bearing outer race.
- Gears: flat back (cone_backing=false), M3 grub screw + M3 nut trap from the back,
  hub boss Ø12 on the front face. Housing has a Ø14 pad opposite each boss with
  `axial_gap` = 0.3 mm → gears cannot slide into the walls (real prints stalled without this).
  A spacer (or M6 washers) between gear back and upper bearing stops the other direction.
- Tooth tip height above pitch base = face_width·cos(pitch_angle) + 1 (NOT sin –
  that bug once left 0.15 mm wall clearance). Cavity = that + gear_clear (2.5).
- Housing split in two halves in the plane of both shafts (x=0), joined by 6 M3
  bolts through a flange; flange is trimmed flush with the bearing-tube ends so
  shaft exits stay open. Print halves split-face down, supports on build plate only.
- Mounting: on the outer side of the input tube (away from the pot), arm near face
  at `arm_y0` (31.5) from the input axis. Arm 20 wide (`arm_w`, across) × 40 deep (`arm_t`, in the shaft plane) – on edge,
  because gravity bends it in the shaft plane. Not boxed in (user's call): each half
  has one flat cheek (`cheek_t` 6) against a side of the arm + a cap (`cap_t` 4) on
  top as end stop (weight goes into the arm end, not the screws). 2 countersunk
  4 mm wood screws per cheek, pattern `screw_pat` mirrored on side B so screws from
  both sides never meet. Cheeks need build-plate supports (they bridge the arm gap).
  No mounting on top of the housing (unreachable).
- `part` selects output: assembly, housing_a, housing_b, gear_out, gear_in, spacer, print_all.
- Coordinate frame: output axis = +Z through origin (pitch base at z=0, shaft goes
  down), input gear on +Y side; `in_frame()` maps children into the input-shaft frame.

## Motor mount (motor_mount.scad) – current design
- Modelled in the arm frame (common.scad): z = rod/motor axis up towards the gearbox,
  y = from the rod towards the arm, x across. `ARM()` maps it to the world (board top
  = z 0, arm near face meets the foot plate at the origin).
- Cradle: extruded profile, motor bore R, full width at the split line (M4 clamp
  bolts + side nut slots), narrowing to the arm seat at `arm_y0`, two cheeks up the
  arm sides with the shared screw pattern. 2 half-ring clamp caps under the motor,
  1.5 mm squeeze gap. Prints standing on its rear end face.
- Foot: plate (4 countersunk board screws) + two parallelogram cheeks = the arm's
  side silhouette up to `foot_len` 50. Arm end cut 30° off square, rests on the plate.
- `motor_z` (rear face position along the arm) is auto: lowest spot that keeps
  terminals/clamps `board_clear` (10) above the board and clear of the foot cheeks
  → currently 52 mm, lowest point 15 mm. Override with `motor_z_set`.
- `part`: assembly, cradle, clamp, clamps_x2, foot, print_all. Echo reports chuck
  clearance, motor height above the board and where the chuck ends.

## Rendering / checks (run after every geometry change)
```bash
# STL export (each part)
openscad -o stl/housing_a.stl -D 'part="housing_a"' angled_gearbox.scad
# preview image (headless)
xvfb-run -a openscad -o preview.png --imgsize=1400,1100 --camera=0,35,-15,62,0,125,400 -D 'part="assembly"' angled_gearbox.scad
```
Verification done so far with temporary `part` values (intersection must be EMPTY):
- gear vs gear: `intersection(){ gear_out(); in_frame() gear_in(); }`
- housing vs gears: `intersection(){ housing(); union(){ gear_out(); in_frame() gear_in(); } }`
- axial stop: shifting a gear 0.25 mm toward the wall → empty; 0.4 mm → contact only
  within the boss radius (never teeth).
Check exported STLs report `Simple: yes`. Look at the preview PNG after changes.
These check files work from a scratch dir with `use </abs/path/angled_gearbox.scad>`.
Motor mount: check the echo (chuck clearance ≥ 4, lowest point ≥ board_clear) and
a side view (`--camera=0,-40,90,90,0,90,600`).

## Constraints / preferences
- Materials: PETG / ASA / nylon, never PLA (heat + steam). Gears run dry, no grease (above food).
- Nothing greasy or wet-sensitive directly above the pot; lowest output bearing 626-2RS.
- Keep everything parametric; keep the BOM + assembly notes at the top of each .scad in sync.

## Open items
- Measure the real drill chuck length (`chuck_len`, assumed 90) – only affects the
  ghost and the echoed chuck end, not printed parts.
- Rigid chuck + 2 bearings in the gearbox: relies on cradle/gearbox alignment on
  the arm; if it binds, a flexible coupler between chuck and rod is the fallback.
- Board stability: the gearbox leans out over the pot – board must be weighted/clamped.
- Paddle design (wooden, maybe scraping the bottom).
- No combined model of the full unit (gearbox + arm + motor mount) yet – both use
  the same arm frame now, so it mostly needs an arm length / pot height parameter.
